# Cyberbane

A reputation overhaul for Cyberpunk 2077. Night City notices what V does and acts on it: crowds get
out of your way, gangs decide whether to respect you or hunt you, and the people you hurt come back
with names and grudges.

**Download:** [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/cyberpunk2077/mods/35847)

**Version 2.8.5.** Works on Cyberpunk 2077 **2.5** and **2.31** with the same download.
What changed in each version: [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

## What it does

- **You build a reputation.** Fights, jobs, chrome and the way you dress all count. Word spreads
  district by district, so you can be a legend in Watson while Pacifica has never heard of you.
- **The street reacts.** People glance, step aside, freeze or run, depending on how frightening you
  are. Cover your chrome or wear a mask and they can't place you.
- **The street makes up its own mind.** Each district keeps its own opinion of you. Never hurt the people there, put
  down the gangers and cyberpsychos who prey on them and leave the beaten alive, and they stop backing away and start
  greeting you, most of all on your own block. Hurt one of them and that district remembers. Earn it in enough districts
  and you are **The People's Merc**.
- **Gangs pick a side.** Help a gang's enemies and they warm to you: better prices on their turf and
  warnings when their enemies come for you. Keep hurting them and they send a crew, then their apex
  squad, by car or on foot. You can pay them off, and each gang haggles in its own voice.
- **Rivals.** Enemies who escape you, or whom you beat and leave alive, come back later with a name,
  a rank, scars from how you hurt them, and their own people. Spare them, kill them, or let them climb.
- **Turf wars.** A gang you keep hurting loses its streets to the gang at war with it. Small outfits like the Mox
  guard their own blocks and only take over a district once everyone else there has been bled dry.
- **A name you earn.** Before the Heist you're a merc few people have heard of. Gangs and Rivals take longer to come
  for you, and no gang sends its apex squad until your Street Cred reaches 15. It grows into the full thing by Street
  Cred 20.
- **Your crew goes to war.** A war on a gang lasts until one side takes terms or is broken. Give the crew an order and
  lead it yourself: they wait for you at the place and the fight starts when you arrive, wave after wave, with your
  squads' people at your side. The gang hits back every few hours; get there in time and that fight is yours too.
- **Messages in your phone.** Gangs, Rivals and street contacts text you, and some threads let you
  answer: pay a gang off, or haggle over where a Rival is hiding. Prefer cards? Keep everything as cards and still get
  your crew's asks (a day off, their cut, a ripperdoc) on the phone.
- **Skip to it.** When something you came for happens at a set hour (a Rival home after dark, a street event), you are
  told when the moment you get there, and can skip straight to it.
- **The Hub.** A Cyberbane tab in the game menu shows your standing, every gang, the streets (who holds each district,
  and where your name carries), your Rivals, your crew, your titles and a history of what you did, as cards you can
  read at a glance, and explains why the city treats you the way it does.

### New in 2.0

- **Talk to anybody.** Walk up to someone with empty hands and press F. Ask questions, buy a Rival's
  hiding place, pay for safe passage, or talk down a ganger who is about to draw. They answer in their own game voice,
  and V says V's own recorded words wherever V has them (more of them with AudioXL, below).
- **Make the street talk.** Grab a ganger, crouch over one you put down, or scan one, and they give
  something up, sometimes a Rival's fatal flaw.
- **Rivals with a life.** Catchphrases, family who take a fallen Rival's place, call-outs to a place
  they name, bodyguards, and mercy as your choice.
- **A living street.** Bagmen to rob, favours for friendly gangs, and sit-downs between gangs at war
  that you can crash (now under **Gang life** in Mod Settings).

### New in 2.8

- **Wars you fight (2.8.5).** Go to war on a gang from the Crew page's **War** view. The war has a front, and both sides
  tire as it drags on. It runs at any hour: pick an order (hit their corners, raid a stash, go after a lieutenant, scout
  them, or hold) and who goes, then **Lead it yourself** (the crew waits for you at the place, near you on the gang's
  streets) or **Send them** (they go in within the hour; join them if you get there first). The gang hits back every
  few hours: your corners, one of your crew out on a job, your home, a price on your second. Lookouts or a scout tell
  you ahead; otherwise you hear as it starts. Be at either and it happens in front of you, in waves, with your crew and a
  few of your squads' people at your side, and what they had on them is yours. The enemy brings what it really has: its
  people, its money, its Rivals and its friends. Wars can be lost.
- **Ending a war (2.8.5).** A beaten gang asks for terms: tribute, its streets, a lieutenant handed over, or the gang
  working for you. Refuse and the last move is storming their boss with your crew. A winning gang demands terms of you.
  Your people can be taken alive: pay, trade one of theirs you hold, or go and get them. Lieutenants you take can be sold
  back or turned. Every war gets a name and a page in History, and the beaten remember.
- **Squads (2.8.5).** From Lieutenant, a member gathers people of their own: up to four, twelve at Legend, growing from
  the streets your crew holds. Each squad has one job: **fighters**, **lookouts**, **hitters**, **hustlers**, **guards**
  or **medics**, and a new one takes a job the crew still lacks. In a war they work together and a few of them fight
  beside you; between wars each has work of its own. They cost wages from the kitty, except hustlers.
- **The crew as a gang (2.8.5).** Put a Lieutenant in charge of each district your crew holds, make a cleared gig place
  your crew's own, and spend the kitty on a training room, an armoury, a second car or a safehouse. Your crew grows from
  a few friends to a power in the city, and veterans can retire alive.
- **Streets by neighbourhood (2.8.5).** A war takes the gang's neighbourhoods one at a time, and gangs clash where their
  people really meet.
- **Hunting while hunted (2.8.5).** A Rival at their place no longer stays away because somebody else is after you.

### New in 2.7

- **Your crew, as people (2.7.7).** Each member has a temperament, a story (where they joined, their scars, their ranks,
  how many they've put down) and asks you for things now and then: a better gun, a day off, a cut, revenge on the Rival
  who hurt them. Make one your second to speak for the crew and lead its wars. When one dies, the crew buries them the

- **Chrome and weapons that do what they say (2.7.7).** Chrome shows what it adds before it goes in, arm chrome makes a

  experimental setting on, they carry it).
- **Your crew's streets (2.7.7).** Shopkeepers in your districts pay the street tax to you in person, the gang that lost a
  district tells you when it's coming back so you can be there, your kills count in your crew's wars, and members hang
  out on the corners you hold.
- **Rivals and gangs (2.7.7).** Calling cards, a history with every Rival on the Hub, humiliated Rivals who turn, and
  Rivals who hunt your crew. Gangs have named bosses who invite you to their sit-downs; crashing a gang's business has

- **Pick a side (2.7.7).** At a sit-down, a deal or a street fight, tell either side "I'm with you" (or open fire on the
  other side when the gang you like invited you). They fight beside you as companions until it's over: your crew leaves

- **Work for the corporations (2.7.7).** A corporation that doesn't hate you texts contracts now and then: one of its
  rivals' people on your map, with two bodyguards. Put them down for good pay and a lot of standing with that
  corporation. **Work for the corporations** in Mod Settings turns them off.
- **Sounds (2.7.7).** Your crew calls out what's coming, hunters shout as they arrive and their cars honk, and loot, pins
  and promotions have their own sounds. **Sounds** in Mod Settings turns them off.
- **Your crew at war, and on the map (2.7.6).** Plan a war: pick the gang and who goes, see the odds, and win by
  strength rather than luck. A win pays: the kitty gets their money, their war chest and the district's own hidden
  stashes go on your map, and your crew gains a hold on their streets. Hold enough and your crew takes the district,
  takes a daily street tax and is talked about everywhere. **Chippin' In**: in an apex fight, one of your crew may get
  you back up from a killing blow. Call or send home one member at a time, rename them, and read each one's own thread
  beside the crew's group chat.
- **Fenix Rebirth, and fairer fights (2.7.6).** With Fenix Rebirth, being brought back counts as dying: your killer
  becomes a Rival and the fight ends where you fell. Nobody appears out of thin air behind you any more, Rivals no longer
  stand frozen in a heap, people you put down stay until you're done with them, and your crew take a seat each in your
  car and get out when sent home.
- **Much Better Contracts, side by side (2.7.5).** Detected on its own. Its contracts are jobs to Cyberbane, it sends
  nobody into a fight while one is running, and each contract you are paid for counts as a finished job. A car you took
  from a gang is never deleted with you in it.
- **Your crew, as you know them (2.7.4).** Each of your crew keeps the look they turned up in, pick lists scroll past ten,
  and you can talk to somebody down at your feet while crouched. The Aldecaldos and the Wraiths come in their own painted
  cars, and the Wraiths keep to the Badlands. Translations no longer crash the game.
- **On time, and on their way (2.7.3).** A gang event's time is given to the minute and it starts when it says; turn up
  early and you are told when they are due. Crews and your own crew find their way to you in tight streets, people on
  foot come in ahead of you while you drive, and ordinary crew members leave their loot on their bodies, not in your
  pockets.
- **Nobody is unkillable (2.7.2).** However tough a Rival, an apex squad or a hunter gets, Cyberbane keeps them within
  reach of your own weapons, counting their armour, and nothing it sends heals while you are hurting it.
- **Places to find a gang (2.7.2).** The Mox at Lizzie's Bar, Maelstrom at Totentanz, the Valentinos at El Coyote Cojo,
  the Tyger Claws at Wakako's, the Voodoo Boys at their chapel and the Animals at the Grand Imperial Mall. The first time
  you are there it goes on your map, and when you come by and none of them are about, a few are there to talk to.
  Anybody of theirs will tell you where it is.
- **The crew at home (2.7.2).** Put up to three of your crew on **Watch the house** and they stand at your building's
  door and car park while you are home. Near your stash, you can give one of them a gun from it, and it stays in the
  stash with all its mods. Talk to any of your crew to ask how they are holding up, what their old gang was like, and
  whether they know anything about a Rival from it.
- **Crew jobs and the kitty (2.7.2).** Send one of your crew on a hit on one of your Rivals or after a gang's bag, with
  their chance shown before you pick. What the crew brings in goes into a kitty that pays their cut; take it when you
  are home, or have one of them run it to your place. The Tyger Claws and the Valentinos sometimes challenge your crew,
  one of theirs against one of yours, and a robbed bag can hold goods to sell to another gang.
- **Rivals worth their names (2.7.2).** A Rival who climbs comes back as one of their gang's signature fighters: mantis
  blades, gorilla arms, a heavy machine gun, a netrunner's deck. A top Rival may take over a place you cleared on a gig,
  with lieutenants and a squad, and wait there for you; kill them and the gang loses it, and what they kept there is on
  the floor to go through.
- **Meetings you can watch (2.7.2).** Turn up early to a sit-down or a deal and watch: they walk in from further out
  now, and if they spot you watching, the street tells you.
- **Home ground (2.7.1).** The building around each of your apartments counts as home: the lobby, the corridors and the
  car park. Nobody Cyberbane sends comes for you there, people already looking for you don't find you there, and no
  bagmen, gang events or street fights start there. A fight that followed you in carries on. Cyberbane learns each home
  the first time you are inside it or sleep there; a house from another mod you mark yourself (see Settings). **Home

- **A moment of peace (2.7.1).** After any fight, everybody rests: no Rival, crew or apex squad is lined up until the

  hours as well.
- **Lose them before they get there (2.7.1).** Once a gang has sent people, changing how you look, putting a mask on,
  leaving the district, riding the metro or going home breaks the trail. One of those and they come in looking in the
  wrong place; two or more and they may lose you before they set off. A gang at Vendetta, one with a tracker on you,

- **What your mask does (2.7.1).** The Standing page says what a mask or a gang's colours are doing for you. It works

- **Ready to translate (2.7.1).** Every line Cyberbane shows is in one file, and a translation installs as a download of

### New in 2.5

Everything added since 2.3.0, in one place. The major changes in each update are in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

**The street**
- **The street reads you.** Witnesses run to tell the gang unless you stop them. Night, rain and your own
  blood change who notices you. Each district has its own temper, and places remember what you did there.
- **Known by your car and gun.** Use the same ones and a mask stops hiding you. A gang's bosses can see
  through a disguise.
- **Wannabes** square up to you for a name, fists only, and the street hears how it went.
- **Pay the street** to keep watch for you, or to forget what they saw.
- **The street tells it.** Lines that know who you are and what you did, the morning brief, the day's headlines and
  your legend on the Hub, voices, panic in a crew you pick off unseen, and answering gangs and Rivals by text.

**The gangs**
- **A code for each gang.** Every gang respects, forgives and fights in its own way, talks like itself, and
  adapts to how you fight it. The Hub tells you each code as you learn it.
- **Gang life.** Funerals, celebrations, initiations, recruiting drives, arms deals and rackets happen at a set time
  somewhere in your district, by one of the data terminals you've found, and the place is pinned on your map. Get
  there, then crash them, pay your respects, or let the gang grow stronger.
- **Gangs fight gangs.** Executions, street fights and hunts you can step into, power struggles where you decide
  who runs a gang, and rival gangers clashing on contested streets.
- **The turf war has money in it.** Gangs have war chests and manpower; back one for a weekly cut, set two on
  each other with a lie, watch rivals unite against you, and at the very top, take streets of your own.
- **Peace with terms**: haggle a gang's price by text, or trade a favour for it.

**Being hunted**
- **Crews with a sergeant**, mixed from the people you've met, and apex squads that hunt you as a team. Talk a
  sergeant down before the shooting starts.
- **Fairer hunts.** A moment's grace when they find you, trails that go cold when you lie low, scouts you can
  stop or pay off before they call you in, and lookouts in the crowd.
- **The hunt grows.** Trackers planted by quickhacks, open season from a gang at the top of its anger, crews lying
  in wait on your road, and the Tyger Claws' assassin.
- **A price on your head.** Gangs, Rivals and the corporations put bounties on you, and named solos from the
  hunters' ladder come to collect. Beat them to climb the ladder, pay the price off from the Hub, or kill whoever put
  it up. The corporations hire killers now instead of sending crews.

**Rivals**
- **Rivals with a story.** A facedown before the shooting starts, humiliation, Rivals back from the dead, Rivals at
  war with each other and bound to each other, trophies, chrome that answers how you hurt them, and last words.
- **Their places.** A Rival keeps hours at a place of their own. One of their people who spots you runs to warn
  them, and if the runner gets away they move a few streets over. Word that a Rival is getting new chrome can tell you
  which ripperdoc they are laid up at.

**Your people**
- **A crew of your own.** Rivals who owe you, gangers you frightened, solos you pay and people a friendly gang lends
  you work for you. Call up to three when you are hunted, and keep them loyal, or one day they sell you out or turn
  on you and come back as a Rival.
- **Friends on the street.** Borrow a friendly gang's people to watch your back, become one of a gang, ransom the
  ones who give up, and feed the street lies.

**The Hub and the map**
- **The Hub in cards.** Every gang, district, Rival and crew member as a card in the game's own style, the gangs after
  you first. Click a gang and every other card shows how it stands with that one.
- **The map remembers.** Pins for a day where a Rival fell, a gathering was crashed, a district changed hands or one
  of your crew died, and, with TweakXL, a CYBERBANE box in the map's filters for all of Cyberbane's pins.

## Install

**Vortex:** add the zip with "Install From File", enable it and Deploy.

**By hand:** extract the zip into your Cyberpunk 2077 folder (the one with `bin` and `r6`). The
scripts end up in `r6\scripts\Cyberbane\`. To update, extract the new zip over the old one.

To uninstall, delete `r6\scripts\Cyberbane` (and, with Night City Allies,
`bin\x64\plugins\cyber_engine_tweaks\mods\NightCityAllies\Interactions\cyberbane_crew.lua`) or disable the mod in Vortex. Your Cyberbane progress
lives in your saves: a save made while the mod is removed loses it.

## How to play

**Your fighting style** is whatever you have done most of, fading slowly, and a new one has to pull clearly ahead to
take over. The History page shows how every style stands. **Butcher**: kills, more in open fights and with explosives,
most for brutal ones (finishers, blades, fists, mantis blades, monowire). **Ghost**: kills nobody sees, and takedowns
out of a fight. **Operator**: putting people down alive in an open fight. **Brawler**: fists, clubs and Gorilla Arms,
knockouts too. **Netrunner**: quickhacks. **Chromed**: fighting with a Sandevistan, Kerenzikov or Berserk running.


- **The Hub:** open the game menu (Inventory, Map, Character...) and pick the **Cyberbane** tab, after
  Journal. With the game's radial menu, open Character and it is beside it. Can't find it? Turn on
  **Hub in the pause menu** (Mod Settings > Cyberbane > Messages and HUD) and it is under Escape instead.
  Click a gang to pay them off, click a Rival to read their file, buy where they are hiding, call them out or forget
  them, and run your crew. The buttons on a page act on what you picked. **Streets** reads the map two ways: who holds
  each district, and where your name carries. The Crew page has five views: **Roster** (pick one of your crew: the first
  button calls just them, **Orders for** them kits them out, trains, renames or retires them, and **Call your crew**
  calls everybody), **Member** (their numbers, slots and story), **War** (your wars, the crew's orders, terms and anybody
  taken alive), **Squads**, and **Crew** (the cut, the kitty and what it buys, who runs your streets, your crew's place,
  its name, colours, home and the fallen). **Show as a list** on a page turns its cards into a list.
- **Skipping time:** when something you came for happens at a set hour and you are there early, a note says when. The
  **Skip to** button on the Hub's first page jumps straight to it, or set a key for it (Mod Settings > Cyberbane >
  General > **Skip time (key)**). It works only where the game itself lets you skip time.
- **Your crew's wars:** on the **War** view, **Go to war** opens a war on a gang. **Give an order**, pick who goes, then
  **Lead it yourself** or **Send them**. After each order the crew regroups for about an hour; with no order for a few
  hours, your second sends one (**Your second runs the wars**, under **Your crew**). The place and its deadline are on
  your map: get there and the fight starts, and your crew comes with you. Their moves show on the map too. **Terms or an
  end** answers their terms, or calls the war off.
- **Talking:** look at somebody up close with empty hands, press F (A on a controller), scroll to pick
  a line. Reach for a weapon and the conversation ends. With a weapon in your hand, the prompt shows
  **Holster to talk** instead, and a greyed prompt also says when somebody just spoke to you, is busy
  fighting or is part of a job. Vendors, joytoys, fixers and ripperdocs keep the game's own conversations.
  While you talk to one of a gang, their people nearby hold their fire, and on a gang's restricted ground the warning to
  leave waits until you are done. Afterwards they are as they were, unless the talk changed that. Hurt one of them and
  the hold is off. On hostile ground, where they shoot on sight, step out first.
- **Calling your crew:** open the **My crew** thread on your phone and pick **Need you. Now.**, press **Call your
  crew** on the Hub's Crew page, or set a key for it (Mod Settings > Cyberbane > Your crew > **Call your crew (key)**).
  Everybody who is free comes, as many as there is room for around you. In a fight they go home once it is over; with
  nothing to fight they walk with you until you send them home (**Go home. I've got this.** on the phone, **Send your crew home** on the Hub, or the same key). To
  call or send home just one of them, pick them on the Crew page and press the first button, or use **Need you. Come
  to me.** and **Head home. I've got this.** in their own phone thread. They come to gigs and side jobs; **Your crew on
  jobs** changes that. Cutscenes, the main story's own missions and safe places like your
  apartment keep them out. If they can't come, or have to leave, they say why. They can be killed like anybody else, and the fallen go
  on the memorial; turn off **Your crew can die** and the game protects them the way it protects its own companions.
  Drive off and they ride with you; the ones your car has no seat for, or who hadn't reached it when you pulled away,
  get a car of their own and follow you, and get out when you do (**Your crew drive after you** turns that off).
- **Your crew at home:** on a member's **Orders**, the Work row goes through earning, recruiting and **Watch the
  house**. Members on watch stand at your building's door and car park while you are on home ground: once you have gone
  in and out of the apartment and parked there, they know where. Stand near your stash and **Give them a weapon** lists
  its guns too. Talk to one of your crew (look at them, press F) for **Hangin' in there?**, **Tell me about** their
  old gang and **Heard anything?**
- **Jobs, the kitty and challenges:** on a member's **Orders**, **Send them on a job** lists the Rivals they can go
  after (from Veteran) and the gangs whose bag they can take (from Soldier), each with their chance. The kitty, and a
  challenge waiting for an answer, are on the Crew page's **Crew** view; the kitty is taken at home or at your crew's
  place. **Running the money** is a kind of work on the Work row. Goods from a robbed bag are sold by
  talking to somebody of another gang.
- **Finding a gang:** ask one of theirs **Where do your people hang out?**, or just go. A gang's home goes on the map
  the first time you are there, hidden while that gang is hostile to you.
- **Recruiting a Rival:** beat a Rival without killing them, look at them where they lie and press F, and spare them.
  The next time you put that Rival down, the same talk offers **[Recruit] You owe me. Work for me.** A Rival you have
  spared twice may also text you, and you can ask them in the thread. Crouching over somebody down is for questioning
  their people; a Rival on the ground is talked to.
- **Your crew's lives:** what a member asks of you (a day off, their cut, a ripperdoc when they're badly hurt) is answered
  from their **Orders**, or in their own phone thread. **Make them your second** is there too. Give a weapon to answer a
  member who asked for a better one; put down the Rival who hurt them for revenge. The **Crew** view can make your
  apartment the crew's home while you stand in it. Shopkeepers in your crew's districts pay you the street tax when you walk up to them. **How hard your crew
  has it** and **Their own weapon in their hands (experimental)** are under **Your crew** in Mod Settings.
- **Sounds:** your crew's, the hunters' and the phone's sounds are under **Sounds** on the Messages and HUD page, beside
  **Voices for the moments**.
- Cyberbane sets no keys of its own. The only one it offers, for calling your crew, is yours to choose.

## Settings

Everything is in **Mod Settings > Cyberbane**. The first page, **General**, has what most people
change: Cyberbane on or off, a preset (Subtle, Default, Dramatic, or one with a shape: Blood feud, Turf war,
Quiet life), where messages go, the hunt line
at the top of the screen, and map markers. The pages after it go deeper, one subject each, with
**Advanced** at the bottom.

- **Fewer interruptions:** set **Where messages go** to Off. Nothing about how the mod plays changes. **Cards, crew asks
  by phone** keeps everything as cards except your crew asking for a day off, their cut or a ripperdoc, which comes to
  your phone to answer there.
- **Words gone before you can read them:** raise **How long words stay on screen**, first on the
  **Messages and HUD** page. It covers messages, phone pop-ups, what you overhear and every line of a
  conversation.
- **Set how the gangs see you:** **How gangs see you** puts every gang, or any one of them, at Allied,
  Friendly, Neutral, Hostile or Vendetta, whatever you have done. **Earned in play** puts it back.
- **Leave a gang alone:** switch it off under **Factions**. Cyberbane stops reacting for that gang
  and keeps your standing for later. The NCPD can be switched off the same way.
- **How often you are hunted:** each gang comes by its own grudge against you, and Rivals by theirs. **How eager gangs
  are** and **How eager Rivals are** under **How often they come** stretch or shorten every wait, **Apex squads** turns
  those off, and **Minutes between encounters** is the breather after each fight, up to two hours of play. With
  **Everybody rests after a fight** on, that breather holds back Rivals, crews and apex squads alike. **Quiet after a
  night at home** keeps them away for a few game hours after you sleep in your own bed, and **Home ground** keeps the
  building around your apartments quiet. **Before you're known**, on the same page, keeps the early game gentler until
  your Street Cred reaches 20; switch it off to be known from day one.
- **Hiding from a crew:** a mask makes every gang slower to find you, and their colours make their people slower to pick
  you out. With **Lie low** on (under **Being hunted**), changing how you look, masking up, leaving the district, riding
  the metro or going home after a gang has sent people can make them lose you before they arrive.
- **Gang homes and guards:** **Gang homes** under **Gang life** switches the gangs' places off. **Guards at home**,
  under **Your crew**, sets how many can watch the house (0 to 3, 0 for none).
- **Your crew at war and on the street:** **Chippin' In** (the chance in each apex fight; 0 is off), **Your crew takes
  streets** and **Your second runs the wars** are under **Your crew**. Wars themselves are on the Hub's Crew page,
  **War** view.
- **Crew jobs, Rivals' chrome and strongholds:** **Crew jobs and the kitty** and **Challenges to your crew** are under
  **Your crew**, **Goods in stolen bags** under **Gang life**, and **Signature chrome** and **Strongholds** under
  **Rivals**. All are on by default.
- **A house from another mod:** stand inside it and turn on **Make where I stand a home** under **How often they
  come**. It becomes home ground like your apartments. **Forget the home I stand in** undoes it. Turn the switch off
  again before you use it somewhere else.
- **Playing with an economy mod:** **How much Cyberbane pays**, under **Reputation and money**, scales every eddie
  Cyberbane gives you: bounties, contracts, jobs, tribute and the cash its people carry.
- **Conduct with quickhacks, burns and poison:** a burn, a poison or a shock you started never costs Conduct when it
  finishes someone you put down or who gave up. **Shooting someone who gave up costs Conduct**, under **Reputation and
  money**, switches off the cost of finishing them on purpose.
- **The street's opinion of you:** each district comes to think well or badly of you by how you treat its people (see
  What it does). **The street makes up its own mind**, under **Reputation and money**, turns it off; then the street
  only ever fears you. Where you stand in the district you're in is on the Hub's Standing page, under the crowd's line.
- **Your crew in cars:** when you drive off, the ones your car has no seat for get a car of their own. **Your crew drive
  after you**, under **Your crew**, turns that off; they walk instead.
- **Fewer notes from street talk:** **Say why someone won't talk**, under **Street talk**, stops the short message about
  why somebody won't talk to you. It never shows during the game's own conversations.
- **Fewer messages:** **How much you hear**, on the first page, spaces out the street's chatter (word going round and
  lines overheard). Warnings, demands, offers, your crew and answers to what you did always come through.
- **Start over:** **Reset all Cyberbane data** under **Advanced** (or Start over on the Hub's History
  page) wipes this playthrough's reputation, and older saves of it are wiped too when they load, so
  loading one does not bring it back. Changed your mind? **Undo the last Start over** on the same page,
  then load a save from before it: its reputation, Rivals, crew and titles are back. Where your Rivals
  were last seen and how hard you had hit each gang are gone either way.

Each playthrough keeps its own record, and nothing one character does reaches another.

## For mod authors

Cyberbane has an extension API (2.7.4), so an add-on can use it without calling Cyberbane's internals, which change
between releases. Everything is on the `CyberbaneAPI` class; guard each call with `@if(ModuleExists("Cyberbane.Api"))`.
A later level adds its own marker module (`Cyberbane.Api2`, ...), so an add-on built for a newer level compiles to nothing
on an older Cyberbane instead of breaking every script mod on the install.

- **API level 1 (`Cyberbane.Api`, 2.7.4):**
  - Listen: extend `CBListener` (kills, Rival events, posture changes, the world settling, Start over with its epoch, a new
    playthrough) and `RegisterListener` each session. Calls arrive on Cyberbane's tick, never inside a game event.
  - Read: `ApiLevel`, `IsEnabled`, `MessagesMode`, `HowMuchYouHear`, `FactionOn`, `Preset`, `BeforeYoureKnownHolds`,
    `GangCodesOn`, `PlaythroughEpoch`, and the older `GetRenown`, `GetDread`, `GetStanding`.
  - Ask: `RequestGroup` (people, through Cyberbane's gates and its 18-spawn ceiling), `RequestConvoy` (a convoy that travels
    a route), `PinPlace`, `RegisterSender` + `SendAside` and `AskPriced` (texts and priced questions), `AdjustStanding` with
    a reason, `RegisterEventKind` + `StartEvent` (Cyberbane's one event slot, its clock and holds), `RegisterGangsRow` and
    `RegisterPage` (cards on the Hub, as data), `MakeFactionProfile` + `RegisterFaction`.
- **API level 2 (`Cyberbane.Api2`, 2.7.5):**
  - Clocks and place: `CBListener.OnTick` (every couple of seconds on the settled tick), `WorldStable`, `ClockWord`,
    `CurrentDistrict`, `PlaceName`, `FindPlaces` (fast-travel points of a district between two distances from V).
  - Sides: `SetGroupsHostile`, `SetGroupsNeutral`, `SetGroupConvoyHostile`, `SetGroupConvoyNeutral`, body to body, kept as
    people arrive and taken back when either side is released. Groups asked for on V's side fight V's enemies.
  - Things in the world: `RequestVehicle` (empty, out of V's sight, optionally wrecked) + `VehicleWhyNot` +
    `ReleaseVehicle`; `DropLoot` (the game's drop bag near V, tier 1 to 5); `MovePin`.
  - Convoys: `ConvoyOutcome` (arrived, broken, got away, released, unknown), `ConvoyAlive`, `ConvoyBodies`;
    `ConvoyState` answers -1 for a handle it does not know.
  - Names and records: `FactionName`, `FactionLabel` (a registered faction's `nameKey` and `labelKey`), `RivalIds`,
    `RivalFaction`, `RivalRank`, `RivalName`, `RivalAlive`, `FileHistory` (a History line that moves no standing; keys
    carry a dash), `StartEventFrom` (an event announced by your own contact).
- **API level 5 (`Cyberbane.Api5`, 2.8.3):**
  - `WithdrawAsk(game, sender, token)` takes back your open question in that contact's thread: its answers neither show
    nor cost anything.
  - `SetFactionListed(game, id, listed)` keeps a faction you show somewhere else off the Gangs list; its standing and
    History stay. For the session: call it on `OnSettled`.
  - A bodyguard: `RequestEscort(game, record, untilFightEnds)` (a group handle), `EscortActive`, `ReleaseEscort`. One
    person, friendly, following V with a seat of their own in V's car; they walk off and are removed out of sight when the
    fight they came for is over (with `untilFightEnds`), when V gets far away, when the story or a safe place takes V, or
    when V shoots them. At most two at a time.
  - Posts: `RequestPost(game, faction, at, count, records)` (a group handle), `ReleasePost`. Up to six people placed
    around the point the way a gang's hangout is, neutral to V until V hurts one of them, and talked to like anybody.
  - Talk topics: `AddTalkTopic(game, faction, lineKey, token, listener)`, `RemoveTalkTopic`, and `CBTalkTopicListener`
    with `TopicShown(game, faction, token, speaker)` and `OnTopic(...)`, which returns what they say back. A line of
    yours in street talk with that faction's people (`n""` for anybody). Both are asked while the talk is on screen: words
    only there. For the session.
  - Also: `RegisterTitle` refuses an id it already has, `FindPlacesIn` finds places in the Badlands' smaller districts
    once V has been near them, and `Cyberbane/HuntEnded` (the hunting faction, 1) comes when a hunt ends.
- **API level 4 (`Cyberbane.Api4`, 2.8.2):** `JoinCrew(game, record, look, name, source, loyalty)` makes a character of
  yours one of V's crew (the member's id, 0 when they could not join; Cyberbane spawns its own body for them from then
  on, so send yours away), and `CrewJoinBlocked(game, record)` says why one cannot join now ("" when they can). CET
  scripts reach the same through `Game.GetScriptableSystemsContainer():Get("CBCrewDoor")`: `CanJoin(record)`,
  `WhyNot(record)`, `Join(record, look, name, source, loyalty)` and `JoinLabel()`.
- **API level 3 (`Cyberbane.Api3`, 2.8.1):**
  - Signals between add-ons: `Emit(name, faction, value)` and `CBListener.OnSignal`, delivered on the tick. Names carry
    the sender's prefix; Cyberbane's own (`Cyberbane/RivalSlain`, `Cyberbane/BountyPosted`, `Cyberbane/LadderRank`,
    `Cyberbane/HuntStarted`) arrive the same way.
  - Listen: `OnNpcKilledBy` (record and rarity), `OnStandingAdjusted` (every `AdjustStanding` with its reason), and the
    Rival event `SlainByV` beside `Slain`.
  - Clocks and place: `ClockHeld`, `PlaceId` (the game's district enum name), `FindPlacesIn`, `IsSandstorm`, `IsNight`.
  - The event slot: `StartEventFromFor` (your own window), `EndEvent`, `EventSlotBusy`, and a fair turn between
    Cyberbane and add-ons.
  - Standing: `PostureOf`, `ReputationFrozen`, `AddPostureLayer` + `RemovePostureLayer` (how a faction acts, without
    touching what V earned), `AddPriceFactor` + `RemovePriceFactor`, `FactionRegistered`.
  - Hunting and money: `ClaimFaction` + `ReleaseFaction` (Cyberbane sends none of that faction's crews), `PostBounty`,
    `CancelBounty`, `BountyOn`, `OfferCorpWork`.
  - Titles and history: `UnlockTitle` and `CBTitleDef.conditionKey`; `FileHistoryFor` (`{0}` is the faction's name).
  - Groups and cards: `CBSpawnSpec.neutralToV` with `GroupSideTaken`; `CBConvoySpec.friendly`; `CBHubCardSpec.order`.
- An add-on's text contact can't share a name with one of Cyberbane's own threads (a gang, a district's broker, an
  unknown number); its texts are refused and the log says why.
- An API function is never removed or renamed; a retired one stays and does nothing. Every change is compiled on the
  game patches Cyberbane supports.
- Keep your own saved data in your own `ScriptableSystem`, your own localization prefix, and your own settings page.

## Compatibility

- **They Will Remember / Night City Remembers:** detected automatically, nothing to set. Cyberbane lets it decide
  who is hostile to you, and the Gangs page marks the gangs it decides with "(TWR)" or "(NCR)". Somebody whose gang
  it has hostile won't talk to you; the prompt says so. They Will Remember also takes over crews and disguises.
  Night City Remembers sends nobody after you, so Cyberbane's crews still come. Set
  **They Will Remember / Night City Remembers** to Off under **Advanced** to let Cyberbane decide instead.
- **Much Better AI, Street Sense, New Game Plus:** detected automatically; Cyberbane steps back
  where they already cover something.
- **Much Better Contracts (Repeatable Gigs):** detected automatically. Its contracts are jobs to Cyberbane: nobody
  Cyberbane sends comes for you at a contract's site, and while a contract is running Cyberbane sends nobody new into a
  fight. Each contract it pays you for counts as a finished job for your Renown; its pay is its own, with no gig bonus on
  top.
- **Fenix Rebirth:** detected automatically, with no dependency either way. Being brought back counts as dying: the killer
  becomes or climbs as a Rival, the death is counted, and the fight ends where you fell. Nothing happens while Fenix is
  bringing you back, and nobody is sent while you're its prisoner. Chippin' In stays off with Fenix installed.
- **Smasher Encounters:** detected automatically. While Smasher is after you, Cyberbane sends nobody new and turns nobody
  on you at sight, and his Arasaka backup doesn't break and run. Your crew still comes when called.
- **Equipment-EX, WEAVE and other outfit mods:** your outfit is read as it looks on V, Equipment-EX's outfits included. A body suit in Equipment-EX's
  body slots covers your arms and legs. With WEAVE, a helmet or mask it tags as covering the face hides yours; WEAVE
  tags every helmet that way, so take the tag off an open helmet in WEAVE's wardrobe if you want your face seen.
- **Lizzie's Braindances:** works alongside. Its braindance seller keeps to her own business, and the Mox still turn
  up at Lizzie's to talk to.
- **Change V's Native Language and Cosmopolitan Night City:** work alongside, and so does playing with the game's voices
  in another language. When V speaks a language other than English, V's English recordings in conversations stay
  silent, and V's own voice lines come out in V's language. The people you talk to answer in the language Cosmopolitan
  Night City gives them, with Cyberbane's words in the subtitles.
- **Night City Allies:** detected automatically, nothing to install or set. A companion with you gets **Join my crew** in
  Night City Allies' own menu. They leave its squad and become one of your crew in Cyberbane, with their name, their look
  and how much they like you. Without Night City Allies the file Cyberbane puts in its folder does nothing (CET notes
  the folder once in its own log).
- **Combat Evolved:** works alongside. Its damage multipliers count when Cyberbane sizes up how hard you hit, so the
  people it builds to last against you still do, and your crew's strength next to yours stays true. It needs its own
  requirements up to date (DigitalVixenCore and RedLogger); an old one stops every script mod from loading, Cyberbane
  included.
- **Phone Extension:** works alongside.
- **Companions** (the game's and modded ones) fight the people Cyberbane sends after you.
- **Story characters, quest NPCs, AMM and romance mods** are never touched.
- **Safe areas:** nothing happens in apartments, clinics, bars and other places the game treats as
  safe. Two switches under **Safe areas and jobs** change that. The building around your apartments is home ground
  (see Settings).

## Translating

Every line Cyberbane shows is in `r6\scripts\Cyberbane\Localization\en-us\English.reds`. To translate it:

1. Copy that file into a folder of your own beside Cyberbane's, for example `r6\scripts\Cyberbane_de\German.reds`, so
   updating Cyberbane never overwrites your work.
2. In your copy, rename `CBLocalizationEnUs` to `CyberbaneText_` and your game language with its dash as an underscore:
   `CyberbaneText_de_de`, `CyberbaneText_fr_fr`, `CyberbaneText_pt_br` and so on. Change the `module` line to one of your
   own.
3. Translate the second text in each line and leave the first, the key, as it is. `{0}` and `{1}` are filled in by the
   game and can go wherever your language needs them.

The top of the file explains the rest. Lines you have not translated yet show in English, so after an update you can
compare your file with the new `English.reds` to find what was added. Rival nicknames are translatable too (`DefineEpithets`): a translation changes only what the
player reads, never who a Rival is, so switching language renames nobody in a save. The log says whether your translation
loaded ("Translation for ... loaded"), and once a launch whether translations can load at all ("Translations can load
here"). Share your translation as a mod of its own on Nexus, with Cyberbane as a requirement.

## Reporting a bug

Post on the mod's Nexus page with your game version, your list of mods, and what you were doing when it
happened. A screenshot helps a lot: the Hub's Standing and Gangs pages say why the city is treating you
the way it is, so a picture of the page that fits your problem often explains it.

- **A red error at start-up** means a script failed to load and Cyberbane is not running. Send
  `r6\logs\redscript_rCURRENT.log` from your game folder.
- **A crash to desktop:** if you have a crash reporter installed (for example Advanced Crash Reporter),
  send its newest report.
- **Anything else, with Cyber Engine Tweaks installed:** send `bin\x64\plugins\cyber_engine_tweaks\gamelog.log`.
  Cyberbane writes what it was doing there, including why somebody would not talk to you.
