# Cyberbane

Cyberbane is a reputation overhaul for Cyberpunk 2077. Night City forms an opinion of V and acts
on it. Fight, finish jobs, install chrome and put a look together, and a reputation spreads
district by district. Crowds clear out of your way, gangs decide whether to respect you or hunt
you, and vendors move their prices. The enemies who got away from you, and the ones you beat and
left breathing, come back with names and grudges. Keep hitting one crew and they stop sending
squads and send an apex hunter, sized to how dangerous you have become.

Every reaction comes from something you did. When you want to know why the city treats you a
certain way, open the Hub: it spells out what each number means, and tells you exactly what the
city is doing about you right now.

**Download:** [CyberBane on Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/cyberpunk2077/mods/35847)

**Status: released (1.4.0).** Built for Cyberpunk 2077 v2.5 and in active development. This
page describes what the mod does and how it installs.

## What it does

- **Reputation you earn.** Two main scores grow from your actions: Renown (how widely you are
  known) and Dread (how frightening you are). Word spreads outward from where things happened,
  so you can be a legend in Watson while Pacifica has never heard of you.
- **A reputation for how you work.** Night City also reads your conduct. Leave people breathing
  when you could have finished them and you get treated as disciplined: vendors give you a
  little goodwill, and the street talks about you with respect rather than fear. Gun down
  civilians, or kill by hand with a blade, and the Street starts talking about you the way it
  talks about cyberpsychos, one bad night from going over. How you kill counts separately from
  how much: a blade and bare hands read as brutal, a gun does not, and a club is its own thing.
- **Word spreads because somebody saw it.** Reputation travels on witnesses. Leave people
  alive and your name moves fast and far; leave nobody and the street never learns who did
  it, so you build almost no reputation you can claim and the story barely leaves the
  district. What you do get instead is a district with a name for something it cannot
  identify. It turns the signature mechanic into a decision you make in every fight.
- **Wear a mask and nobody can say it was you.** People still see what happened, they just
  cannot put your name to it, so your reputation barely grows, no story about you travels, and
  gangs are slow to turn against you. Nobody scatters from you in the street either: whatever
  you are known for, a stranger has no way to attach it to the person walking past them. The
  bodies still turn up, so the district gets frightened of something it cannot identify.
  Anonymity costs you fame, which is the trade. Glasses and visors do not count; they only hide
  the chrome in your eyes.
- **Anyone the mod sends after you is on your map.** Rivals, apex squads and crews all carry a
  marker on the map, on the minimap and above their head, so you can pick the one that matters
  out of a street full of gangers. There is a setting for turning it off.
- **You overhear the city talking about you.** Lines change with the gang, the district and the
  title you are carrying, and when a story about something you did has actually travelled to
  where you are standing, that is what people are talking about.
- **The street gets word back to you.** When a rumour about you reaches somewhere new, a
  contact may get in touch about what they heard. If people saw you, your name is in it. If
  nobody did, it arrives as a question about who is doing this.
- **A city that reads how dangerous you look.** The more frightening you are, the more people
  back off. Civilians, gang members and even cops can notice you, cower, or flee, and panicked
  drivers pull away in the street. It is not only your reputation: visible chrome, and how
  built-up your level, Street Cred and skills are, all feed into how much the street fears you,
  so a heavily chromed, high-level merc clears a room. Bosses and MaxTac hold their ground.
- **What you wear hides what you are carrying.** Chrome only frightens people who can see it.
  A coat covers your arms and skin, trousers cover your legs, and anything across your face
  covers your eyes, so the same build reads very differently dressed up or stripped down.
  Covered chrome still counts for a little, because a coat moves and heavy arms change how you
  carry yourself.
- **Gangs with their own values.** Maelstrom cares about chrome, the Animals care about raw
  strength, the Voodoo Boys care about netrunning. The same V gets different treatment from each
  gang depending on your standing and reputation: respect, a warning, or hostility. Masks and hoods
  make you harder to place, and wearing a gang's colours makes that gang slower to recognize you.
- **Enemies that break, run, and remember.** Wound an enemy badly and it can lose its nerve and
  try to escape. Catch it and that is the end of it; let it get away and it comes back as a named
  Rival with a grudge. Beat one down and walk away without finishing them and they can come back
  too, taking the mercy personally. Rivals are a full nemesis system: they hunt you down and
  chase you, they climb the ranks on their own while you are busy elsewhere, and every time one
  escapes it returns a rank higher and scarred. Each one is built to the same grade as a member
  of an apex squad, and they keep getting stronger: every escape, every mercy and every time
  they put you down makes the one who comes back harder than the one who left. Each arrives
  with a bar at the top of the screen carrying their name. Put down the one hunting you and it
  is finally finished. Anyone who kills you can also come back, and dropping a crew's member
  can bring a gang-mate gunning for you.
- **They drive over.** A hit squad or a gang's apex comes by car, dispatched and driven by the
  same thing that sends police after you, so it uses real roads and keeps coming across
  junctions. The crew gets out and comes for you, and if you are behind the wheel it gives chase.
  An apex squad keeps its shape in the car: a heavy, a shooter, a blade and a netrunner. Indoors,
  or anywhere there is no room for a car, they come on foot.
- **A vendetta is a decision, not a dice roll.** A gang that has sworn one is already putting a
  crew together, and the Hub tells you how long you have. How many turn out depends on how much
  of a problem they think you are, and they bring the gang's apex squad with them. Pay them off
  while they are on the way and they turn around.
- **The day has a shape.** A day in Night City is split into three stretches of eight hours,
  and each kind of trouble gets its own window inside them, staggered so a Rival and an apex
  squad sharing a stretch arrive hours apart rather than together. Three Rivals means one in
  each stretch; crews and apex squads take two stretches each, and which ones is decided fresh
  every day, with the exact moment inside a window left to chance so it never becomes a
  timetable you can learn. How many of each per day is up to you. Sleeping or fast travelling
  past a window simply misses it, so you never arrive somewhere and get everything you were
  owed at once.
- **You can lose them.** Being hunted is a chase you can win. Get far enough from all of them
  and stay clear long enough and they give up, the same way the police in this game do. A Rival
  you shake takes it personally and comes back promoted, so getting away still costs you
  something.
- **A city that pays attention.** Frighten a gang enough and they may send tribute to stay on
  your good side, or a hit squad to deal with you. Give a hit squad the slip and a survivor can
  come back as a named Rival who hunts you. Vendors give fear or respect discounts, and fixers pay
  the famous a bonus on job payouts. You can also buy your way out of a vendetta by brokering peace
  with a gang from the Hub, and the price reflects what they actually think of you: the bodies,
  how badly their standing has fallen, and double again if they have sworn a vendetta. Paying
  them off calls off anything they already had on its way.
- **Gangs send an apex squad.** Keep putting one crew in the ground and they stop sending
  squads and send the people who matter: a carload of four named enforcers who fight like
  MaxTac. There is a heavy who walks at you and breaks your guard, a shooter who flushes you
  out of cover, a blade who closes on a Sandevistan, and a netrunner who hangs back and takes
  your weapon off you. Each chair is filled with a real member of that gang you have actually
  fought, so they carry the right weapons and fight the way that kind of enemy fights, and they
  work as a squad rather than four individuals. Each of them gets a bar at the top of the screen
  showing their role and their gang, so a squad reads as a heavy, a blade, a netrunner and a
  shooter rather than four of the same, and you can always tell whose people you are fighting.
  You get a warning first, and a countdown. How dangerous they are is a slider, from a hard fight
  up to the game's own set-piece enforcers, and your own build scales it on top. Each one
  carries two traits of its own, so no two fight the same way. No story character is ever used,
  so nothing gets spoiled and you never meet a duplicate of someone you already killed. It
  works for every gang. The Hub keeps count of the ones you put down.
- **Gangs you cannot decapitate.** Every gang has a leader and up to three lieutenants, and
  your Rivals fill those chairs. Put the leader down and the strongest of the rest takes the
  crew, so there is always somebody next in line. A leader takes real killing; a lieutenant
  takes more than the rank and file.
- **Rivals carry your marks.** Set one on fire and the one who comes back is burned. Shoot
  them, cut them, or fry them with a quickhack and they wear that instead, and the Hub shows
  it on their line.
- **Jobs count.** Finishing gigs, jobs and NCPD work builds your reputation, with bigger
  contracts worth more than street hustles.
- **Titles and the file they keep on you.** Earn titles such as Butcher of Watson, Chrome
  Saint, or the top title Cyberbane. The Log tab is the street's file on you, put together
  from what people actually witnessed, so work nobody saw never reaches the page.
- **The Hub.** One full-screen page built to look like the game's own menus, same font and
  colours, opened with F7 by default (rebindable).
  Your scores are meters, gangs and rivals are colour-coded rows, and the first screen shows
  what each of the three lanes has on its way, with a live countdown to when it will reach you.
  If something cannot arrive, it says why rather than showing a number that is not moving. Each
  press steps through tabs for your reputation, the factions, the districts, your rivals, your
  titles, and the log; the press after the last tab closes it. Gangs show how long they have
  been quiet or how long a vendetta has been running, districts show how long since you were
  last busy there, and the log keeps a running tally of what the city has on you. Every screen
  explains what its numbers mean rather than throwing abbreviations at you, and the first one
  tells you exactly what the city is doing about you right now and what it would take to
  change that.

Cyberbane works anywhere in the open world. It adds no hand-placed scenes, and it is built to
leave quests, companions, romances and your saves alone.

## Requirements

Install these first, each from its own page and following that mod's instructions:

- RED4ext
- redscript
- Codeware
- Mod Settings

There are no optional requirements. Cyberbane reads your clothing through the game's own
equipment slots, so outfit mods are picked up without any integration.

Cyberbane is built for Cyberpunk 2077 v2.5. Install the 2.5 versions of the required frameworks
above. Each game patch is treated as unsupported until it has been checked, and a compatibility
note goes out with every release.

## Installation

Download the latest `Cyberbane-x.y.z.zip` from the
[Releases page](https://github.com/tfthushaar/CyberBane/releases), then install it one of two ways.

### With Vortex (recommended)

1. Install the required mods listed above.
2. In Vortex, add the zip with "Install From File" (or drag it onto the Mods area), then enable it
   and click Deploy.
3. Launch the game and press F7 to open the Hub.

### Manual

1. Install the required mods listed above.
2. Extract the zip into your Cyberpunk 2077 game folder, the one that holds `bin`, `r6` and
   `Cyberpunk2077.exe`. The zip's `r6` folder merges into the game's existing `r6` folder, so the
   scripts land at `r6\scripts\Cyberbane\`. A typical Steam path is
   `...\steamapps\common\Cyberpunk 2077\`.
3. Launch the game and press F7 to open the Hub. You can rebind the key in Mod Settings.

To uninstall, remove the `r6\scripts\Cyberbane` folder, or disable the mod in Vortex.

## Controls

Cyberbane adds one key, the Hub key, with two shortcuts layered on it. Everything else uses the
game's normal inputs.

- **Hub key (F7 by default):** opens the Hub, then steps through its tabs one press at a time; the
  press after the last tab closes it. Rebind it in Mod Settings. It will not open during combat.
- **Shift + Hub key (while the Hub is open):** brokers peace with the worst gang you can afford to
  buy off, paying from your eddies on the spot.
- **Ctrl + Hub key (while the Hub is open):** cycles your active title through the ones you have
  earned, or back to Auto (which always shows your highest title).

## Uninstalling

Cyberbane makes no permanent changes to the game, so removing it stops every effect right away.

Cyberbane stores its progress inside your save. If you save the game while Cyberbane is not
installed, that save loses its Cyberbane data for good. Keep the mod installed to keep your
reputation, or make a backup save before you remove it.

## Settings

Everything is configurable from the in-game Mod Settings menu, and every option explains what
it does. You can turn individual systems on or off (crowds, gangs, morale, vendors, rivals, hit
squads, whispers), tune how intense and how frequent reactions are, pick a preset (Subtle,
Default, or Dramatic), and rebind the Hub key.

Three worth knowing about if a playstyle is being treated unfairly:

- **How many of each kind reach you per day**, set separately for Rivals, gang crews and apex
  squads. Turn one to zero to switch that kind off entirely.
- **How frightening unseen work is.** By default half of a killing nobody witnessed still
  counts toward how frightening you are, because the bodies turn up either way. Turn it down if
  you work quietly and do not want the street reacting to you, or to zero so only what people
  actually saw counts.
- **Whether melee kills cost Conduct.** Killing by blade, bare hands or takedown counts against
  you only when somebody sees it. Turn it off if how you kill should be your own business.

## Compatibility

- They Will Remember: supported, detected exactly and automatically. Cyberbane backs off the
  systems that overlap (hostility on sight, hit squads, faction-colour disguises), keeping the
  rest. Night City Remembers is treated the same way, since it replaces They Will Remember. You
  can force the compatibility mode on or off yourself in Mod Settings.
- Street Sense: supported, detected automatically, no setup. It owns crowds noticing and
  greeting you, so Cyberbane stands its own overheard lines down and keeps everything else.
- Much Better AI: supported, detected automatically, no setup. Its Nerve system owns morale,
  so Cyberbane stands down its own morale break and terror push rather than panicking the same
  enemy twice, and it also owns equipping enemies, so Cyberbane leaves its own ability kits off
  rather than handing the same enemy a second Sandevistan. It keeps tracking who got away, so
  Rivals still come from enemies Much Better AI sent running, and everything that is Cyberbane's
  own still runs: who gets sent after you, when, in what, and the boss bar.
- Responsive NPCs: works alongside. If crowd reactions feel doubled, lower civilian intensity or
  disable one side's crowd reactions.
- Night City Alive and other crowd or patrol mods: supported.
- AMM, romance and companion mods, quest NPCs and unique characters: always left untouched.
- Safe areas: in places the game itself treats as safe, such as apartments, clinics, bars and
  memorials, Cyberbane's reactions stand down and nobody there responds to you. Nobody is sent
  after you in one either, because the game forces your hands empty in those places and that
  would be a fight you could not have. If you want it anyway, two settings under Safe areas
  turn it on and lift the weapon lock, both off by default.
- New Game Plus: supported, detected automatically, no setup. It rebuilds every NPC in the game
  to be harder, and its buffs and Cyberbane's would multiply rather than replace each other, so
  Cyberbane halves its own apex buffs and leaves the random traits to New Game Plus, which
  already hands them out. Starting one also starts a fresh reputation for the new playthrough.
